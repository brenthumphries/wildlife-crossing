## Purpose

A running list of minigame concepts for Wildlife Crossing, prioritized for
alignment with the project's educational goals: teaching players about the Y2Y
(Yukon to Yellowstone) corridor and the wildlife and ecology it represents. All
concepts assume the cozy, no-harsh-fail-state tone of the core game and the
literal Y2Y setting described in [[game-design-overview]].

Two batches are recorded below. The first is a general top-ten. The second is a
set deliberately kept in a *different mechanical register* from the core loop
(Survey → Select → Build → Observe → Earn → Unlock), so they don't duplicate the
main game's verbs.

---

## Batch 1 — Educational top ten

1. **Crossing Architect** — Design a wildlife overpass or underpass over a
   highway, place funnel fencing, and watch grizzlies, elk, and cougars learn to
   use it while roadkill drops. The game's namesake mechanic; models the real
   Banff Trans-Canada crossings and teaches habitat connectivity.
   *(Note: overlaps heavily with the core build loop — see Batch 2 for
   non-overlapping alternatives.)*

2. **Path of the Pronghorn** — Guide a pronghorn along its ~150-mile migration,
   threading bottlenecks and development while learning that pronghorn go *under*
   fences, not over. Dramatizes a signature Y2Y migration and teaches corridor
   protection and wildlife-friendly fence retrofits.

3. **Hyperphagia** — Play a grizzly in autumn racing to build fat before
   hibernation, foraging whitebark pine nuts, berries, and army cutworm moths
   while avoiding human food and conflict. Teaches bear ecology and the "a fed
   bear is a dead bear" coexistence message.

4. **Trophic Cascade** — Command a wolf pack and watch the downstream ripples:
   elk move off the willows, aspen and streambanks recover, beavers and
   songbirds return. Turns the Yellowstone wolf-reintroduction science into
   playable cause-and-effect.

5. **Beaver Works** — Place dams to raise the water table, flood a meadow into
   wetland, and unlock knock-on benefits for amphibians, fish, and moose. Casts
   the beaver as ecosystem engineer and teaches the keystone/wetland concept.

6. **Native Waters** — Manage a mountain stream by removing invasive lake and
   brook trout, reconnecting culverts, and keeping water cool for native bull and
   cutthroat trout. Introduces aquatic connectivity and invasive-species
   pressure.

7. **The Nutcracker's Cache** — Play a Clark's nutcracker caching whitebark pine
   seeds across the alpine; forgotten caches sprout new trees while blister rust
   and beetles threaten the stand. Teaches a real mutualism and the decline of a
   high-elevation keystone tree.

8. **Burn Season** — Plan controlled burns to trigger lodgepole pine's
   fire-dependent (serotinous) cones and stitch a mosaic of habitat ages across
   the valley. Teaches that fire is a regenerative force, not pure destruction.

9. **Bear-Proof** — Secure a campground or ranch before nightfall: latch bins,
   store food, electric-fence the beehives, and clear attractants to keep bears
   wild. Turns everyday coexistence practices into a quick, replayable puzzle.

10. **Field Survey** — Identify species from camera-trap photos, tracks, and scat
    to log biodiversity and populate the in-game encyclopedia. Mirrors how real
    Y2Y biologists monitor the corridor and doubles as an on-ramp to educational
    reference content.

## Batch 2 — Non-overlapping with the core loop

Each of these uses a mechanical verb the core loop never touches (recognition,
triangulation, deduction, measurement, memory, drawing, rhythm, dialogue), so
none duplicate the Survey/Select/Build/Observe/Earn/Unlock cycle.

1. **Camera-Trap ID** — A motion-triggered photo (sometimes blurred, half-frame,
   or nighttime IR) pops up and you name and log the species. Verb: *recognition*
   — the sim tracks populations for you but never asks you to tell species apart.
   Teaches real field appearance and seeds an encyclopedia.

2. **Signal Chase (radio telemetry)** — Rotate a directional antenna, read signal
   strength, and triangulate a collared wolverine from three bearings. Verb:
   *geometry/triangulation*. Teaches how biologists actually locate animals
   across the corridor.

3. **Track & Sign** — Match footprints, gait patterns, and scat to the animal
   that left them. Verb: *matching/deduction*. Teaches field sign — lynx vs. wolf
   track, grizzly dig vs. black bear — grounding the roster in observable
   evidence.

4. **Trailhead Encounter** — You round a bend and meet wildlife; pick the right
   response under gentle time pressure (make noise, back away slowly, don't run,
   give a cougar space). Verb: *decision/reflex*. Teaches human-wildlife
   coexistence; framed as safe-behavior learning, never gore or punishment.

5. **Snowpack Survey** — Dig a snow pit, read the layers, and judge whether spring
   snowpack is deep and late enough for a wolverine to den. Verb: *measurement*,
   tied to climate. Teaches the wolverine's snow dependence and why a warming
   corridor threatens it.

6. **Phenology Journal** — Log first-bloom, first-thaw, and first-arrival dates
   through the seasons, matching observations against a naturalist's calendar.
   Verb: *observation timing*. Teaches phenology and how shifting timing ripples
   through the food web.

7. **The Nutcracker's Memory** — As a Clark's nutcracker, watch seeds get buried,
   then recall the cache locations later from memory. Verb: *concentration/
   memory*. Teaches the whitebark pine–nutcracker mutualism.

8. **Naturalist's Sketchbook** — Trace and shade a species to add it to your
   field journal, with the game highlighting diagnostic features (a bighorn's
   horn curl, a lynx's ear tufts). Verb: *drawing*. Teaches morphology and
   rewards close looking; very much in the Stardew register.

9. **Call of the Corridor** — A *rhythm/audio-memory* game where you echo elk
   bugles, wolf howls, and loon calls back in sequence. Opens a new sense the
   core loop doesn't use; teaches animal communication and rut/territorial
   behavior.

10. **Knowledge Keepers** — A dialogue-and-matching vignette that pairs
    traditional ecological knowledge with the right season, species, or
    stewardship practice. Verb: *knowledge/story* — distinct from the permissions
    trust-meter that governs unlocks; it educates rather than gates. Should ship
    only through the mandatory cultural-advisor review in
    [[cultural-narrative-design]], using the same fictional, region-specific
    communities.

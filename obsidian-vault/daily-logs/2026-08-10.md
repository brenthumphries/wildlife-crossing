## Decisions made

- **Windows binaries ship unsigned, deliberately and visibly. macOS is signed
  and notarized. Every artifact is covered by a detached OpenPGP signature over
  a SHA-256 manifest.** Brent's call, recorded in ADR 0018. Azure Artifact
  Signing was investigated first and rejected on three counts, not on price: it
  cannot sign from macOS (SignTool plus a Microsoft dlib, Windows only, and the
  official GitHub Action runs on Windows runners only), its identity validation
  is quoted at 1 to 20 business days and would gate the release date, and Public
  Trust certificates restrict individual developers to the United States and
  Canada. What it buys is the partial softening of one dismissible SmartScreen
  interstitial, since a non-EV certificate accrues reputation over time.
  Recurring cost falls from about 219 USD/year to 99.
- **macOS signing runs on Brent's Mac, not in CI.** No signing credential of any
  kind enters GitHub. With the Windows route dropped that is true by
  construction rather than by policy, since nothing is left that a CI job would
  need a secret for. Rejected: `rcodesign` on `ubuntu-latest`, which needs the
  Developer ID certificate, its password and the App Store Connect key stored as
  repo secrets.
- **The macOS export target changes from `.zip` to `.dmg`.** A notarization
  ticket cannot be stapled to a zip, and the zip is why `LICENSE` and
  `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md` land beside the archive instead of with the app.
- **The DCO check requires the trailer's email to match the commit author.**
  `CONTRIBUTING.md:58` already asked for it and nothing enforced it. Without the
  comparison, `Signed-off-by: Somebody Else` passes and the attestation is
  decorative. Display names are not compared, since people legitimately change
  how their name is spelled.
- **A stdlib script rather than a third-party DCO action.** The repo uses only
  first-party `actions/*`, and the gate guarding contributions is the wrong
  place to take on a supply-chain dependency.
- **V2's boot gate covers Windows, not macOS.** Decided on who can check what by
  hand: the macOS build is launched and watched during every visual QA and
  signing session (`signing-runbook.md` A8), and nobody here owns a Windows
  machine, so without the job the `.exe` reaches players having never been
  executed anywhere.
- **The pack gate reports every platform before failing.** `set -e` would stop
  at the first bad pack. `smoke_boot.sh` already argues the other way about its
  two boots, and three platforms diverging in three ways is one report worth
  reading.
- **C4's measurement uses paired runs from identical seeds, sampled inside the
  run.** Death is absorbing (`_step_agent` sets `alive = false`, nothing revives
  an agent), so measuring "after" in the same run compares against a population
  the baseline already thinned. Totals at t=2000 say almost nothing: every arm
  converges on "most of them died". The first two attempts reported a 4% and
  then a 1.4% mortality reduction and would have written "the crossing achieves
  nothing" into the design memo. Sampling within the run shows 6.30 against
  1.20 at one in-game day.
- **The tutorial camera finding is flagged, not fixed.** Where the first view of
  the game points is a design call.

## Open questions / blockers

- **Four commits are unpushed:** `792b237` (DCO gate), `d7a2a61` (V2 pack gates
  and the Windows smoke job), `ed60d1c` (C4 measurement), and the commit
  carrying this log with its build-review amendments. Working tree clean, all
  signed off. Push via [push-runbook](../../docs/push-runbook.md), then
  `tools/ship.py --verify`.
- **The tutorial's opening camera points at the dead zone.** `main.gd:18` sets
  `CAMERA_FOCUS_COORD = Vector2i(13, 6)`. Row 6 has zero measured deaths across
  10 seeded runs and logs about 10 crossing uses against row 1's 192. 70 of 73
  baseline deaths happen on rows 0 to 2. One-line change, owner's call.
- **Rendered agents never respawn.** `_spawn_agents()` runs once in
  `load_world`; `_step_agent` sets `alive = false` permanently. `PopulationModel`
  recovers counts monthly and nothing re-derives agents from them, so the
  visible world empties out over about 20 in-game days regardless of what the
  player builds. Phase 1 separated observable agents from demographic counts by
  design (ADR 0009); re-seeding from recovered counts is the missing other half.
- **63% of visible animals die in the first in-game day**, roughly ten real
  seconds at 1x, before the player can have built anything. A tone question
  against the "cozy, not stressful" north star, currently inherited from
  `EnvConfig.DEFAULT = 0.20` rather than chosen.
- **`smoke-windows` is unverified until it runs.** It assumes
  `actions/download-artifact` restores
  `builds/wildlife-crossing-windows-x86_64/wildlife-crossing.exe`, and it is the
  first time Git Bash's `timeout` and `stdbuf` get exercised by
  `smoke_boot.sh`. Both failure modes annotate rather than mislead.
- **The `dco` job is unexercised.** It is `if: github.event_name ==
  'pull_request'`, so pushing to `main` will not run it. Worth a throwaway pull
  request carrying a deliberately unsigned commit before it guards anything.
- **Signing prerequisites are not started.** Apple Developer Program enrolment
  (99 USD/year, a few days) and the GPG signing key are both step one of
  `signing-runbook.md` and neither exists yet.
- **C4 covered Bow Valley only.** The original question was raised about the
  other 17 segments, which do not bisect and where a safe detour does exist.
  Nothing measured says anything about them.
- **B1 and C3 are still open**, unchanged by today: no export of `HEAD` has been
  launched or looked at, and no written visual QA note exists.

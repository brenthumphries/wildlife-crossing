24 entries covering the species you're working for, the regions you'll unlock, and the mechanics underneath both — each one explaining how it behaves in-game and the real science it comes from.

Species

The eight animals of the launch roster.

  • Bighorn Sheep Bighorn sheep are cliff-dwelling mountain ungulates of the Y2Y corridor, famous for the massive curled horns of the rams.
  • Canada Lynx The Canada lynx is a snow-adapted boreal forest cat whose fortunes rise and fall with its primary prey, the snowshoe hare.
  • Elk Elk are large, gregarious deer of grasslands and forest edges, among the most numerous large herbivores in the Y2Y corridor.
  • Gray Wolf The gray wolf is a wide-ranging social carnivore and keystone predator across the forests and grasslands of the entire Y2Y corridor.
  • Grizzly Bear The grizzly bear is a wide-ranging omnivore of the Y2Y corridor's forests and alpine meadows, and a flagship species for connectivity conservation.
  • Mountain Caribou Mountain caribou are the deep-snow ecotype of woodland caribou, an endangered icon of the inland temperate rainforest and old-growth ranges of the central and northern Y2Y corridor.
  • Pronghorn The pronghorn is North America's fastest land animal and the sole survivor of an ancient endemic family, ranging the sagebrush and grasslands of the southern Y2Y corridor.
  • Wolverine The wolverine is a powerful, far-ranging mustelid of high alpine and subalpine country, one of the most connectivity-dependent species in the Y2Y corridor.

Regions

The twelve sub-areas of the corridor, ordered south to north.

  • Greater Yellowstone Greater Yellowstone is the southernmost sub-area of the Y2Y corridor, anchored by Yellowstone and Grand Teton National Parks and the Wind River Range (WY/MT/ID).
  • High Divide The High Divide links the Greater Yellowstone ecosystem to the wildlands of central Idaho and Montana, across the Centennial Mountains and Big Hole Valley (MT/ID).
  • Salmon–Selway–Bitterroot The Salmon–Selway–Bitterroot sub-area encompasses some of the largest wilderness in the contiguous United States, including the Frank Church–River of No Return Wilderness and the Bitterroot Range (ID/MT).
  • Cabinet–Purcell The Cabinet–Purcell sub-area spans the Cabinet Mountains, the Purcell Trench, and the Highway 95 corridor where Montana, Idaho, and British Columbia meet (MT/ID/BC).
  • Inland Temperate Rainforest The Inland Temperate Rainforest sub-area covers the Columbia Mountains, the Selkirks, and Kootenay Lake — a globally rare wet-belt forest far from the coast (BC).
  • Crown of the Continent The Crown of the Continent centres on Glacier and Waterton Lakes National Parks and the Flathead Valley, where Pacific-moist, prairie, and alpine ecosystems converge (MT/BC/AB).
  • Central Canadian Rockies The Central Canadian Rockies sub-area covers Banff, Jasper, Yoho, and Kootenay National Parks and the Bow Valley (AB/BC) — and is where Wildlife Crossing begins.
  • Peace River Break The Peace River Break is a low-elevation pinch point in the corridor, where the Hart Ranges, the Peace River canyon, and Highway 97 constrict wildlife movement (BC).
  • Muskwa–Kechika The Muskwa–Kechika Management Area is a vast, largely roadless mountain wilderness in northern British Columbia, named from the Muskwa Ranges and Kechika Basin.
  • Upper Liard Basin The Upper Liard Basin spans the Liard Plateau and Cassiar Mountains where British Columbia meets the Yukon (BC/YT).
  • Stikine–Nass–Skeena Headwaters The Stikine–Nass–Skeena Headwaters — the "Sacred Headwaters" — is the shared birthplace of three major salmon rivers, on the Spatsizi Plateau of northern British Columbia.
  • Greater Mackenzie Mountains The Greater Mackenzie Mountains form the northern terminus of the Y2Y corridor, spanning the Mackenzie and Selwyn Mountains across the Yukon and Northwest Territories (YT/NWT).

Mechanics

The concepts the simulation is built on.

  • Connectivity Connectivity is how well habitat patches are linked into a network animals can move through safely.
  • Patch (Habitat Patch) A patch is a contiguous zone of ecologically compatible terrain — the basic unit of habitat in Wildlife Crossing.
  • Segment A segment is the smallest selectable unit of road or barrier in Wildlife Crossing — the thing the player chooses when deciding where to build a crossing.
  • Trust Trust is the progression currency of Wildlife Crossing: a per-entity measure of how much a governing body believes in the player's stewardship.

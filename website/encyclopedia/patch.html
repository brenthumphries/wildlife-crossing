Mechanic

Patch (Habitat Patch)

A patch is a contiguous zone of ecologically compatible terrain — the basic unit of habitat in Wildlife Crossing. Quality, population, and connectivity are all tracked per patch.

In-game

Each patch gets a habitat-quality score (0–100) from four inputs: terrain suitability, patch size, connectivity to other patches, and an edge penalty for perimeter adjacent to roads, urban zones, or barriers. Scores surface as four qualitative bands — Poor / Fair / Good / Excellent — with exact numbers revealed only after a habitat assessment purchase. A patch below its species' minimum viable size cannot sustain a population on its own and slowly declines until linked into a larger connected network — making connectivity mechanical, not cosmetic.

Real-world basis

The patch concept comes from landscape ecology, where habitat is modelled as patches in a matrix, and patch size, quality, and isolation determine whether populations persist. Habitat fragmentation breaks large patches into smaller, more isolated ones — the core problem the game asks the player to reverse.
